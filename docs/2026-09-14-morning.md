> [!abstract] Overview
> The weekend’s dominant macro driver is a fresh Gulf supply shock: a commercial vessel was hit in the Strait of Hormuz, Houthi forces reached a key island, and Saudi Arabia shut its East-West crude pipeline after drone attacks, while planned Iran-Gulf talks to reopen the strait slipped and an Iran-Oman understanding does not provide for immediate reopening. At the same time, hotter-than-expected US August CPI pushed the Fed and other G7 central banks toward a widely telegraphed rate-hike repricing, even as President Trump demanded lower rates and clashed with his handpicked Fed chair. A 5% Treasury yield is now straining risk appetite, and Asian equities retreated on AI slowdown warnings while oil advanced. BRICS leaders countered with new local-currency payment commitments and a joint Gulf restraint statement, but the near-term cross-market thread remains energy-driven inflation colliding with a hawkish G7 policy stance. Asia’s developing importers face a $7 billion gas bill and China’s property overhaul is squeezing local land revenue, keeping Asia FX and China fiscal easing in play.
