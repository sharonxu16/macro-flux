> [!abstract] Overview
> The global bond selloff extended further into Asia hours, with the 30-year Treasury yield breaking to the highest since 2004 and Japanese 10-year yields reaching a 30-year high while New York Fed President John Williams said another rate hike by year-end is reasonable. The dollar clung near its two-month peak as yield spreads between US Treasuries and emerging Asia bonds approached record levels, threatening capital outflows. Oil whipsawed, climbing back toward $105 before easing on renewed US-Iran diplomacy and the restart of a Saudi pipeline, leaving energy as an inflation wildcard. The Xi-Trump summit further narrowed China's geopolitical credit discount and was accompanied by record pre-summit port traffic and a 53% surge in Hong Kong exports. The cross-market thread remains US nominal tightening dominating Asia assets, with trade de-escalation a partial offset and oil supply risk keeping central banks hawkish.

### 📌 Global bond selloff hits long-end and Asia spreads

**Fact**
Yields on the US government’s longest-dated bonds climbed to the highest level in more than two decades, the latest milestone notched in an extended bond selloff driven by inflation and fiscal concerns ([BBG 1](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/us-30-year-yield-hits-highest-since-2004-as-bond-selloff-deepens)). Japanese 10-year government bond yield rose to a 30-year high on Thursday, following a surge in Treasury yields ([CNBC 1](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/24/japan-jgb-bond-yield-treasurys.html)). New York Fed’s Williams said it’s "reasonable" to expect another rate hike by year-end ([CNBC 2](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/24/feds-williams-another-rate-hike-by-year-end.html)). The surge in Treasury yields has pushed yield gaps with emerging Asia bonds toward record levels, raising the risk of capital outflows from the region ([BBG 2](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/treasury-yield-surge-pushes-spreads-versus-asia-near-extremes)).

### 📌 Xi visit narrows China credit discount as trade front-loading accelerates

**Fact**
Chinese ports had their busiest week in history ahead of the approaching summit between Presidents Donald Trump and Xi Jinping, extending a boom in shipments abroad in a possible sign exporters were rushing goods out of the country as uncertainty hovered over tariffs ([BBG 1](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/china-s-trade-volumes-hit-record-in-week-before-trump-xi-summit)). The Trump-Xi summit is narrowing the geopolitical discount on China dollar credit while rekindling overseas risk appetite, according to Bloomberg Intelligence ([BBG 2](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/trump-xi-summit-narrows-china-credit-s-geopolitical-discount-bi)). The recent strength in Hong Kong’s exports extended into August, with the value jumping by `53 per cent` to `HK$667.9 billion` (`US$85.6 billion`), the second-highest level on record after July’s peak, on strong demand for artificial intelligence-related products ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/hong-kong-economy/article/3368645/hong-kong-exports-surge-53-hk6679-billion-second-highest-record)). > [!info] [AI Reasoning]
> * **Narrative change**: Confirmation, because the truce extension and summit optics are now visible in record export front-loading and tighter China credit spreads.
> * **Transmission / Market Read**: Capital-flow and risk-premium channel supports **long China dollar credit** and a more stable **CNH**, with Taiwan risk remaining a regional overlay.
> * **Watchpoint / Confidence**: Xi-Trump joint statement on trade and Taiwan security outcomes; lack of deliverables would reverse the credit narrowing; Confidence: Medium.
