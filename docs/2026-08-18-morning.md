> [!abstract] Overview
> Escalating US-Iran tensions dominate risk pricing after Washington let the 60-day ceasefire memorandum lapse, threatened military action against Oman, and triggered renewed Iranian escalation warnings. Brent rose above `$90` and the US diesel crack hit a record `$102.20`, lifting oil-linked inflation risk just as a separate US-Canada tariff deadline adds another supply-shock layer. The resulting repricing is clearest in long-end rates: the 30-year Treasury yield topped `5.31%`, the highest in 19 years, while the dollar slipped on reduced Fed hike bets. Asian assets are diverging in this environment, with Korean equities catching a bid even as global investors hedge tariff and energy exposure. The common thread is a supply-shock repricing across oil, fiscal issuance, and trade policy that is pushing inflation premiums higher while pressuring growth-sensitive assets.
