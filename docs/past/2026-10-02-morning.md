> [!abstract] Overview
> Risk appetite recovered only narrowly as an escalation in the Middle East oil supply shock collided with a tentative stabilization in government bonds. Crude climbed after Washington deployed an additional carrier, while Europe faced US pressure to release diesel reserves and Moscow halted diesel exports until sanctions lift. Fed officials, including Vice Chair Philip Jefferson, leaned against an October hike, pulling Treasury yields back from multiyear highs and lifting equities. But the long-end stress remains live; eurozone and French borrowing costs surged, and a key U.S. rate blew through a decades-old record. The common thread is a supply-risk and term-premium shock: energy and fiscal supply threaten to keep inflation and yields elevated even as the Fed turns patient.
