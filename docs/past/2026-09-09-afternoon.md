> [!abstract] Overview
> Brent traded above `$100/bbl` intraday and European gas hit its highest since 2023 as US-Iran strikes extended and Strait of Hormuz shipping traffic remained below its 10-day average, dragging equities lower and lifting the yen on haven demand. Washington signed orders banning Canadian dairy, motorcycles and most alcoholic beverages, formalizing a second trade-war front just as Ottawa’s `$20bn` retaliation takes hold. The bond complex stayed unsettled with UK long-dated borrowing costs near multi-decade highs and UK food inflation expected to top `6%` next year, while US Treasury commentary hardened against yen shorts and Fed officials showed openness to fewer policy meetings. China’s producer inflation topped estimates while consumer prices met forecasts, and offshore RMB borrowing hit a record above `1 trillion yuan`. The common thread is a simultaneous war-driven supply shock, trade escalation, and bond stress tilting portfolios toward real assets, yen, and short duration.
