> [!abstract] Overview
> Escalating US-Iran strikes have pushed Brent through `$100/bbl` for the first time since July, with an oil tanker hit in Iraqi waters and one third of Gulf oil still missing despite dark crossings, turning a military headline into a physical oil supply shock. In parallel, the Treasury’s upsized `$6bn` long-end buyback landed at the low end and failed to stop the rise, sending 10-year Treasury yields to the highest since 2023 and tightening financial conditions into major central bank decisions. Rate-hike risk is being repriced sharply across the Fed, ECB and BOE, while energy-sensitive equities fell on the oil spike. US-Canada trade escalation moved from threats to dated import bans on Canadian alcohol, dairy and motorbikes effective `Sept. 29`. The connecting macro thread is a policy-constrained supply shock: oil and Treasury yields are rising together, compressing risk appetite and raising the cost of every central bank’s next decision.
