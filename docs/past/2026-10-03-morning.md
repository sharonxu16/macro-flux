> [!abstract] Overview
> The dominant macro driver overnight was the weak US September payrolls report, which slashed market pricing for a Federal Reserve hike in October and delivered the dollar’s worst day in a month. Emerging-market assets and equities found relief as rate fears cooled, though global bond markets remain stressed after a week of heavy selling. On the supply side, the G7 agreed to release up to 100 million barrels of oil and diesel from emergency reserves, taking the edge off fuel shock fears and prompting Washington to back away from its diesel export ban threat. Eurozone inflation hit a three-year high of 3.8 percent, reinforcing ECB tightening pressure even as France’s bond market strains worsen. The single thread connecting today’s price action is a temporary easing of the two dominant risks, Fed tightening and fuel supply, which favors EM Asia FX and risk assets in the near term, while European fiscal-inflation stress remains the unresolved tail.

## 🔬 Narrative Watch

### 📌 US payrolls miss triggers Fed October hike unwind

**Fact**
The US added fewer jobs than expected in September and wage growth slowed, signaling some caution among employers amid rising costs ([BBG 1](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/us-firms-add-just-29-000-jobs-unemployment-rate-ticks-up)). Nonfarm payrolls rose by just 29,000 in September, well below the 84,000 forecast, and the unemployment rate rose to 4.2%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/jobs-report-september-2026.html)). Nonfarm payrolls increased by 29,000 in September after downward revisions to the prior two months, according to Bureau of Labor Statistics data ([BBG 2](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-10-02/em-assets-find-relief-as-us-jobs-data-eases-rate-fears-video)). The dollar posted its worst day in a month on Friday after a report showed the US added fewer jobs than expected in September, briefly leading traders to waver on bets the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates again this year ([BBG 3](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/dollar-falls-as-traders-pare-fed-hike-bets-after-soft-jobs-data)). Traders now see little chance of a Fed rate hike in October after weak jobs report ([CNBC Markets](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/fed-rate-hike-odds-decline-after-september-jobs-report.html)). Payrolls were weaker than expected in September and unemployment also ticked higher ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/906051fc-c116-4803-b395-2d56d1bcbf28)).

> [!info] [AI Reasoning]
> * **Narrative change**: Reversal. October Fed hike odds collapse on a below-forecast payrolls print, breaking the prior rate-hike repricing narrative.
> * **Transmission / Market Read**: Policy and capital-flow channel. Favors **long EM Asia FX** and **short USD** near term.

### 📌 G7 releases 100 million barrels as US retreats from diesel export ban

**Fact**
The Group of Seven nations and its partners plan to release as much as 100 million barrels of emergency oil and diesel stocks, capping a week of mounting pressure from the Trump administration to bring down soaring fuel prices ([BBG](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/g7-to-release-up-to-100-millions-of-barrels-of-diesel-and-oil)). Group of Seven countries agreed on Friday to release 100 million barrels of diesel and crude oil from emergency reserves and pledged to refrain from energy export restrictions after pressure from US President Donald Trump ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/united-states-canada/article/3369626/trump-says-europe-agrees-release-diesel-stocks)). US backs down from fuel export ban threat as G7 agrees to release 100mn barrels; Donald Trump calls decision by European nations to release diesel from strategic reserves a ‘great thing’ ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/97200b07-755c-40ce-a50b-b51666bd4b7e)). UK inflation could near 5% if President Donald Trump stopped US exports of diesel, economists warned after motorists saw prices hit £2 ($2.7) per liter for the first time ever ([BBG 2](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/economists-warn-of-5-uk-inflation-if-us-diesel-ban-goes-ahead)). 国际油价2日下跌 ([Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261003/0d476a5310784e1d92829f577b3c42bd/c.html)).

### 📌 Eurozone inflation 3.8%, French yields squeeze ECB

**Fact**
Eurozone inflation hits three-year high of 3.8% ([FT 1](https://www.ft.com/content/6394fdc7-5fa5-4ec3-8bde-52633acd2b57)). Annual euro zone inflation hit 3.8% last month, its highest level since September 2023 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/eurozone-inflation-ecb.html)). After two consecutive rate rises, a December move remains our base case even after headline CPI climbed to 3.8% in September ([FT 2](https://www.ft.com/content/e637cd4c-415d-431c-a857-95d6adc33157)). The European Central Bank risks being pulled into the vortex of France’s bond-market troubles as surging yields raise pressure on officials to act ([BBG](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/ecb-s-nightmare-scenario-gets-closer-as-french-bond-yields-surge)). Fuel price support and rising inflation put pressure on countries’ public finances ([FT 3](https://www.ft.com/content/d2f01dd2-2fde-4d3b-aff0-f82aa9e05296)).

> [!info] [AI Reasoning]
> * **Narrative change**: Confirmation. Eurozone inflation-fiscal stress intensifies with `3.8%` CPI and French spread pressure.
> * **Transmission / Market Read**: Policy and risk-premium channel. Favors **long Bunds vs OATs** and **long EUR vs USD** with periphery risk.
> * **Watchpoint / Confidence**: French fiscal response and ECB speakers; wider spreads would signal policy paralysis. Confidence: Medium.
