> [!abstract] Overview
> Escalation around the Strait of Hormuz dominated markets as Iran attacked hostile targets and formally sought to bar US and Israeli vessels from the critical waterway, lifting oil and crushing emerging‑market currencies. Fed hawks tightened the squeeze with St. Louis President Musalem openly favoring a rate hike and Kevin Warsh reportedly ready to move in September, while jobless claims held below 200,000 for a third straight week. The US announced 15 % polysilicon tariffs and a halt to key metal waste exports, yet China bought at least 10 additional soybean cargoes, preserving a fragile truce ahead of a possible Xi‑Trump summit. The overriding thread is a collision between a rising Middle East risk premium and a Fed that is increasingly unwilling to look through inflation — a mix that keeps the dollar bid and EM assets under pressure.

## 🔬 Narrative Watch

### 📌 Hormuz deal stumbles as Iran attacks, demands exclusion of US ships

**Fact**
Oil extended gains after a report that Iran attacked “hostile targets” in the Strait of Hormuz, with Tehran seeking to bar US ships from the critical waterway in a deal with Oman ([BBG 1](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-aug-7)). Iran will seek to bar US and Israeli ships from the Strait of Hormuz and require compensation from hostile countries before they are allowed to use it, according to local media reports on a proposed Iran‑Oman deal to manage the crucial waterway ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/middle-east/article/3363226/iran-seeks-bar-us-ships-hormuz-deal-oman-advances)). The proposed Hormuz passage deal is not feasible for the shipping industry, sources said ([Reuters](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiwwFBVV95cUxQajNmWTdWUHQ1RkRPd0FjWUUzSkd5cTNhN1NxREdIUUxGZklfbGEycml6emw1VThlRXk3YU5aakNULU5DanUwclZDWEpLUTg0c0xjRElxaTlKU2NiRHJzT1JiVmltOTVlQ1hNcGkxaTNjYndSX29leFl6S1l0OGxJd0hfSGZKOEwzZ3JVOURWVGgtY2JWUDZ2aXFoTWNDZV83eXllTko1WjVCSmpCb2IwM0ZNTTRTdDdxaXJGOHhZS2laUTg)). ### 📌 Fed hawks sharpen; Musalem and Warsh signal September hike

**Fact**
Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis President Alberto Musalem said that with inflation running above the central bank’s `2%` target, policymakers cannot afford to tolerate higher inflation while they wait for the possibility of stronger productivity growth ([BBG 1](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/fed-s-musalem-calls-for-meaningful-restraint-on-inflation)). Musalem also explained why he expressed a preference to raise rates at the FOMC’s July meeting ([BBG 2](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-08-06/fed-s-musalem-expresses-preference-to-raise-rates-video)). If future weeks’ inflation data is strong, Warsh will prepare to raise rates in September, according to a report ([WallstreetCN](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiU0FVX3lxTE12VUlFM2FGTFQ1eUdfX1dhN19fZ3pyN28xSUJ0bWNIR29BTk05WWhOQndZTWpnNDJOUDJfMl83QmdoZGVseTdVdjBiVlNQSTFrelE0)). Applications for US unemployment benefits were little changed, remaining below `200,000` for a third straight week and underscoring the labor market’s resilience ([BBG 3](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/us-initial-jobless-claims-remain-stable-below-200-000)).

> [!info] [AI Reasoning]
> * **Narrative change**: Acceleration — the number of FOMC participants openly backing hikes is growing, reinforced by tight claims.
> * **Transmission / Market Read**: Policy channel favors **higher front‑end UST yields**, a **stronger USD**, and pressure on **gold** and **equities**.
> * **Watchpoint / Confidence**: US July nonfarm payrolls tonight; a print above `80K` with steady wages would cement September hike expectations. Confidence: High.

### 📌 US ramps up China trade curbs as soybeans cushion tensions

**Fact**
The United States has announced new tariffs of `15%` on polysilicon — a key material for solar panels and semiconductors — including price floors aimed at challenging China’s dominance ([SCMP 1](https://www.scmp.com/news/us/article/3363224/us-sets-15-polysilicon-tariff-price-floors-challenge-chinas-dominance)). Separately, the US will halt exports of key metal waste in a push to break reliance on China ([WSJ](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMizAFBVV95cUxNNmxXbGU1RGZpYjBMTHl5TEZseWpKVjh3N1d3cDdaTFFlUGFPb0xpWEdYLWdKbWRMVnc5YmtqS1ZSV3htdXN1bkRGQ3F3MW1jUGEtMi1RZUpYRWUxdmFtcEEycHVNN3VmNVpiWWEyTGUzU2ZBR1l5Nkk5ZjNnaFhDM3RsQXYzclBfSlM5bDJhRTJxMnJmWHZDV25TdXBOMy1uRXNWYkFST3pfQ3ZSNVNvRGRRekd2XzlyYnc1RGctc2RubUNRQW5Lb1pEdng)). However, China has bought at least another 10 cargoes of US soybeans, traders said, the latest in a flurry of purchases ahead of President Xi Jinping’s expected US visit next month ([SCMP 2](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/united-states-canada/article/3363218/china-buys-least-10-more-cargoes-us-soybeans-traders-say)).

> [!info] [AI Reasoning]
> * **Narrative change**: Acceleration of trade curbs, but soybean buying provides an off‑ramp — mixed signals.
> * **Transmission / Market Read**: Supply‑chain risk premium supports **long solar/rare‑earth equities** versus **short exporters**; agricultural easing is neutral for now. 
> * **Watchpoint / Confidence**: Any White House comment on the soybean purchases or Xi visit date; Confidence: Medium.
