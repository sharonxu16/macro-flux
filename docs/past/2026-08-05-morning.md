> [!abstract] Overview
> The dominant macro driver is the accelerating expectation of a diplomatic deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, as signaled by US Treasury Secretary Bessent, collapsing the oil supply-risk premium and triggering a cross-asset rally. The S&P 500 and Dow hit records, Treasury yields fell, and emerging-market currencies rebounded, while the dollar weakened on fading rate-hike bets. The US-Yen intervention narrative intensified, with Bessent’s push for a Fed foreign repo facility marking a new era of currency activism, adding another layer of dollar weakness. The convergence of a potential Hormuz deal and a coordinated push to weaken the dollar creates a powerful cross-market thread: the rapid unwind of the energy-shock hedge and the Japan-driven dollar short.

## 🔬 Narrative Watch

### 📌 Horsemen of the Hormuz Deal: Risk-On Rally as Bessent Flags Imminent Accord

**Fact**
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz could be announced as early as Tuesday or Wednesday, adding that the talks would provide “freedom of movement” for commercial shipping ([CNBC 1](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/04/bessent-says-there-may-be-deal-tuesday-or-wednesday-to-open-strait-of-hormuz-with-freedom-of-movement.html)). President Donald Trump has warned that the latest round of negotiations with Iran are the “last chance” to bring an end to the five-month conflict ([CNBC 2](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/04/us-iran-war-trump-hormuz.html)). Bessent’s comments sent oil prices sliding more than `5%` and pushed the S&P 500 to a record high ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/1ed68c71-e1e8-4ae5-a965-5380cbdeaa22)). Treasury yields also fell as the prospect of an interim deal eased inflation concerns and reduced the odds of the Federal Reserve raising interest rates further ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/articles/hormuz-opening-uncertain-after-new-strike-as-trump-administration-says-deal-may-be-close), [CNBC 3](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/04/treasury-yields-investors-peace-talks-uncertainty.html)). The rally in risk assets was broad, with most emerging-market currencies gaining on Tuesday and Asian stocks tracking Wall Street higher ([BBG 1](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/emerging-markets-stay-under-pressure-as-iran-impasse-lifts-oil), [2](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/stock-market-today-dow-s-p-live-updates)). Oil futures fell to a three-week low, with Brent crude dropping well below `$80` per barrel ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cpw9v0gnzxwo)). However, complete certainty remained elusive as Iran has demanded control over inbound traffic in the Strait while granting outbound oversight to an international body ([Reuters](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMivAFBVV95cUxOSjFmUC1OVjJCS05weXNFTmhaZkRab0dwSVV1LXJ5dEp2ZVNxX0l6QVdLQTN3SUdtSS1pOVF4OEYxRzZHbUJNeXk5ZmJnUG5FVVFScEJnOEUyM3FLWWhzQk8yME1VcW42X3dPOURqRi03QlN6RnZLMGFfWW5LWUVmUDctVDR0YXRhWTAwbTJRaUdKYTNJb2NuS2hrNjRpOVdlZjBPRTVzNXhUbjRpdmRFSGdkVHlGUnpJYjRGTQ)). In a separate chokepoint disruption, an Indian-flagged commercial ship sank in the Red Sea on Tuesday after an attack near Yemen, with the Yemeni government blaming Iran-backed Houthi rebels for the “unprovoked” aggression ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/south-asia/article/3362980/indian-ship-sinks-red-sea-after-attack-near-yemen)).

> [!info] [AI Reasoning]
> * **Narrative change**: Acceleration of the Iran de-escalation narrative, shifting from talks to an imminent deal with direct supply implications.
> * **Transmission / Market Read**: Risk-premium channel collapsing favors **long equities**, **short Brent crude**, and **long Gold** as rate-hike bets evaporate.
> * **Watchpoint / Confidence**: Signature and implementation of a formal deal; any Hormuz vessel attack would reverse the move. Confidence: High, driven by primary Bessent comments and broad market confirmation.

### 📌 Bessent’s Yen Activism and the FIMA Push: A New Era of Dollar Weakness

**Fact**
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s intervention to support the Japanese Yen signals a new era of US “currency activism,” showing Washington is ready to scupper trades that push against its interests ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/1be83506-b897-4c26-97cc-445d7354ee6f)). His parallel call to upsize the Federal Reserve’s foreign repo (FIMA) facility to give allies like Japan more dollar liquidity is sparking debate about whether such a move is risk-free ([Investing](https://www.investing.com/news/forex-news/bessents-call-to-upsize-fed-foreign-lending-facility-may-not-be-riskfree-4834725)). The joint US-Japan Yen defense effort has been described as a “perfect storm rising” in FX and bond markets, directly targeting dollar strength and challenging the Fed’s inflation-fighting stance ([Reuters](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMizgFBVV95cUxQOVhFamJnNjNYd1piUGFvMUR6WV9tSWVOdkdaa1lsYmd2UEp3blppbXVKYnZma2E5YjBHQWV1MlJoQjNSZFlQMW04RWRMSEQ3Ykl6Um9DNzJOTFBvSkxuMTVNSC1xWlpzbmtJZFpRMm5NWkxSUFZZRGlpcmpXYjBrNmJHR19UendSQUJzRElnZEtBal94dUUtVlhjN1pacWZNZE10Vlp3Qjk3ZTZzd3ZlZG5FWDROSkUybHNUZE1LamlkYm1heFFFNEhTVEF4UQ)). The news that the Trump administration has refunded around `$100 billion` in “liberation day” tariffs that were struck down by the Supreme Court further complicates the fiscal backdrop ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/b0f728aa-1e71-4bb0-b50f-2e0818712734)).

> [!info] [AI Reasoning]
> * **Narrative change**: Acceleration of a coordinated US-Japan strategy to weaken the dollar and provide liquidity, expanding beyond FX into Fed balance-sheet policy.
> * **Transmission / Market Read**: Liquidity and policy channels favor **long JPY**, **short USD/JPY**, and a **bearish USD** against Asian FX on a broadening activist stance.
> * **Watchpoint / Confidence**: Any official Fed response to the FIMA proposal; failure to endorse it could stall the dollar’s decline. Confidence: Medium, given the political complexity and lack of an official Fed statement.

### 📌 US Missile Stockpile Depletion Exposes Defense Vulnerability

**Fact**
The US military has used up nearly `80%` of key missile interceptors, with commanders warning that stockpiles are “dangerously low,” according to anonymous sources ([CNN](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiggFBVV95cUxQZTYwajFVVUFzVk5nOTJndUdaU2MxRUJyR3B6enBuWk5sWnBUVEtmbFFBVjhZV2V4YjJsdHBLUkVsOFh1dFI4aGxrUUhHcEpKc05relRWWm42MEcybkZFTnlEOE1EM0d5alN1TzMyQ0hHNWRZYVFSZzBJRDNURWNVSzZB)). The US Army has also used “virtually all” of its stockpile of highly accurate long-range precision missiles, principally Army Tactical Missile Systems (ATACMS), during the five-month conflict with Iran, raising concerns about military readiness for future conflicts ([Reuters](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiuAFBVV95cUxOZjVoa01lTFhQYVMtVjRxRWZKSng1YzFUTTNJOVI1T1pWZVNKc1czOWhJWHB3VDFFWnVrY3lLT1FnSVBfU1M1RFdrU2FsYVlKZXdhVm9VbF9PaEFNeGVMR0ZtNmlEeGhkWG1iang1U0kzb3hmWENGSnNXODRzdnZYaE1LYkY4Vm1hbzJEcEl1ZzZIRVBQLS1nYUYyTWdDdjZjXzN0NmtuZm5IV2FEdlBQby1UUnBqc0FF)). The depletion of both offensive and defensive weaponry occurs as the US pivots to diplomacy with Iran, presenting a paradox of strained military capacity alongside a push for de-escalation.

> [!info] [AI Reasoning]
> * **Narrative change**: New, revealing a significant depletion of US military readiness that could constrain future foreign policy options, which may paradoxically reinforce the current diplomatic push.
> * **Transmission / Market Read**: Risk-premium channel favors **long defense equities** on guaranteed restocking demand, but a pullback from a broader geopolitical conflict supports a narrower **short Brent** position on the Iran deal catalyst.
> * **Watchpoint / Confidence**: Any Pentagon statement formalizing the level of depletion; a new supplemental funding request would accelerate the narrative. Confidence: Medium, relying on anonymous sourcing but with high cross-source confirmation.
