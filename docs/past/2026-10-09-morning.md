> [!abstract] Overview
> Oil reprised its role as the dominant macro driver, with Brent spiking more than 4% on renewed tanker attacks, Iranian threats to block additional Hormuz routes, and Hurricane Isaias cutting Gulf of Mexico production before fading after President Trump said the US would not attack Iran before November’s midterms. Beijing pushed back on European claims that an undervalued yuan drives its trade surplus as EU officials opened last-ditch car-export talks, keeping currency and tariff channels live for Asia FX. The rates complex showed tentative stabilization: Fed minutes signaled no urgency for an October hike, ECB officials favored a proportionate response to energy spillovers, and European asset managers argued French bond fears were overdone even as US mortgage rates hit their highest since 2023. The connecting thread is an energy-price shock feeding inflation-sensitive central bank caution while Beijing and Brussels fight over trade imbalances, leaving commodity currencies and China-linked FX at the center.
