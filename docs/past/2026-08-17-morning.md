> [!abstract] Overview
> The weekend's dominant macro driver is the tightening physical and financial squeeze around Iran: tanker attacks, a possible historic oil spill, slowing Hormuz traffic, and explicit US exploration of secondary sanctions on Chinese banks, shadow fleets and exchange houses keep crude bid and risk premia elevated. Washington is separately ordering a scale-back of joint military drills with South Korea after Seoul declined to help denuclearize Iran, while South Korean President Lee proposes formal peace talks with the North and the DMZ remains volatile after warning shots. Softer US data and reduced Fed hike bets are pressuring the dollar just as global rate expectations rise elsewhere, complicating the bond outlook. China's yuan internationalization push gains another foothold with Libya's planned CIPS membership, reinforcing the sanctions-avoidance channel. Monday's China July activity release is the next domestic Asia catalyst. The single cross-market thread is a geopolitical risk premium that supports energy and precious metals while leaving Asian FX vulnerable to both Iran escalation and US alliance policy shifts.
