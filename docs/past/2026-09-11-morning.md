> [!abstract] Overview
> The dominant macro driver is an intensifying Middle East supply shock: Iran-backed rebels have seized a Red Sea port and the Houthis are pushing toward Bab al-Mandeb, while Saudi Arabia has cut output to its lowest this year under a declared maritime embargo. Brent has surged beyond `$108` and US crude is back above `$100`, feeding a global inflation impulse that lifted US producer prices by the most in three months and pushed market-implied Fed hike odds for next week toward `70%`. The ECB delivered a hawkish `25bp` hike to `2.5%` and signaled October is live, while Treasury yields climbed toward `5%` after the buyback undershot. Equities fell, gold sold off, and Asian stocks and bonds opened under pressure. The single cross-market thread is an oil-led inflation shock forcing a hawkish re-rating of global rates, tightening financial conditions and raising the bar for risk assets and rate-sensitive FX.
