> [!abstract] Overview
> Fed Governor Waller’s signal that he is inclined to hold rates in September reversed the early-week rate-hike pressure, sending the dollar to its weakest since May, lifting stocks and bonds, and pushing the yen through `156`. The yen move is amplified by rising BOJ hike and intervention expectations even as Japanese households cut spending for an eighth month. Separately, Iran’s missile and drone attacks on Kuwait keep a floor under oil, with US crude up more than `9%` this week and European diesel cracks at record highs. Tonight’s August payrolls and next week’s CPI now form the two key waypoints for the Fed repricing. The cross-market thread is a tug-of-war between easier Fed policy expectations and an energy-driven inflation tail risk.

## 🔬 Narrative Watch

### 📌 Waller tilts to a September hold, dollar sinks and global yields fall

**Fact**
Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said his next decision on interest rates will be “heavily influenced” by August inflation data due next week ([BBG 1](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/fed-s-waller-says-september-rate-decision-hinges-on-august-cpi)). He said he is “inclined” not to lift borrowing costs at the central bank’s September meeting ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/0a332e3c-e596-4f64-976a-d7746f91a9a9)). In remarks that seem to contrast with statements last week from Chairman Kevin Warsh, Waller expressed confidence in the current inflation trends ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/03/fed-governor-waller-indicates-he-will-support-holding-rates-steady-at-september-meeting.html)). The dollar sank to the lowest level since May after Waller noted progress on inflation and as the yen surged against its major counterparts ([BBG 2](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/dollar-falls-to-weakest-since-may-on-waller-comments-yen-surge)). Stocks and bonds rallied after the dovish Fed comments ([WSJ](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMikgFBVV95cUxON3hVMWlxbFFiWnBrRjNhc2g1ekVTOEsxT0NyRGU2ZkZSenBBYWxVbGFzTjl1TERRb1ZhV3NYR2dzWk80aVFGNlNXcnVmNHVEWkFhSzVVWXNzUXZOcE1mUFV6dFZXR3UyYUZyQ0pzYzJWZVVjY2VEUURBcUhiTjN5dDhDMUVXOFZFdlhUcjI2MEZsZw)).

> [!info] [AI Reasoning]
> * **Narrative change**: Confirmation versus the prior global bond selloff reprieve; a top Fed official explicitly leans toward a September hold.
> * **Transmission / Market Read**: Policy and rates channel supports **long USTs**, **short USD**, **long gold**, and **long Asia FX** into CPI.
> * **Watchpoint / Confidence**: August CPI is the trigger; a hot print would invalidate and revive hike fears. Confidence: Medium.

### 📌 Yen breaks 156 on BOJ hike and intervention bets

**Fact**
The yen surged as traders bet on Japan interest rate rises, moving past `¥156` to the dollar after a sudden jump overnight ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/22abbb77-1344-4871-9692-97a779b1ba77)). The Japanese currency jumped to a one-month high as traders weighed the chance of further intervention, while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC on Monday he believed Japanese authorities would take action to lead to a stronger yen ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/03/yen-japan-intervention-boj.html)). Japanese households cut spending for an eighth consecutive month as rising costs for daily necessities prompt consumers to pare discretionary purchases ([BBG](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-03/japan-s-households-cut-outlays-for-eighth-month-amid-inflation)).

> [!info] [AI Reasoning]
> * **Narrative change**: Acceleration of the prior BOJ hike narrative; yen through `156` even as household spending weakens.
> * **Transmission / Market Read**: Carry and rate-differential channel supports **long JPY** and **short USD/JPY**; may pressure carry-funded positions if volatility rises.
> * **Watchpoint / Confidence**: Watch for BOJ/MOF intervention evidence or official pushback. Confidence: Medium due weak consumption data conflicting with policy bets.
