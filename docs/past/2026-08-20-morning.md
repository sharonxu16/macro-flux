> [!abstract] Overview
> The dominant macro driver of the window was the US Treasury’s surprise decision to double long-bond buybacks, which crushed long-end yields, sent the dollar to a three-month low, lifted gold toward `$4,500`, and helped global equities recover. The move came as the July FOMC minutes revealed a more hawkish committee: many officials favored an increase and said policy tightening would be warranted if inflation failed to decline, setting up a direct tension between official inflation-fighting guidance and the administration’s urgency to suppress borrowing costs. Meanwhile, Washington escalated economic warfare against Iran with what it called the most crushing sanctions yet, the UAE severed economic ties with Tehran, and oil extended a fifth day of gains. The Canada tariff pause and China’s unchanged LPR were secondary. The cross-market thread is a tug-of-war between liquidity-friendly Treasury intervention and hawkish central bank guidance, with an energy risk premium layered on top.
