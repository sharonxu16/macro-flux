> [!abstract] Overview
> The dominant afternoon driver is an oil market caught between fresh kinetic escalation and nascent diplomacy: Brent backed off the six-month high near `$117` and slid over 2% as reports of US-Iran contacts and proposed Mecca pact talks met continued Houthi attacks on Saudi pumping stations and Riyadh airport. A parallel hawkish DM policy repricing kept front-end rates firm, with Fed coverage pointing to quarterly tightening and the BOE signaling its most hawkish stance since inflation was double digits, while the euro-zone slowdown keeps the ECB patient and the yen regains its haven bid. Singapore added a regional energy-security dimension, agreeing to store Saudi crude while factory output cooled sharply in September. The cross-market thread remains a collision of energy-led inflation risk and tight DM policy that keeps Asia defensively positioned, with Treasuries and the yen still the main havens and spot crude vulnerable to sharp two-way swings on any diplomatic breakthrough or infrastructure strike.
