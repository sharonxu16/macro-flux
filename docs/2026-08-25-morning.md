> [!abstract] Overview
> The dominant macro driver is the US Treasury’s newly explicit `$950 billion` war chest, which sharpens the bond-buyback/fiscal-debasement narrative, lifts gold toward three-month highs, and keeps the dollar under pressure even as Wall Street questions whether buybacks can durably cap long-end yields. A parallel escalation in the US-Iran financial offensive, branded `Economic D-Day`, widens the sanctions net to any Iran-linked entity while oil slips on renewed diplomacy and contradictory physical transit signals. Simultaneously, the US-Canada trade war jumps higher with a threatened `50%` tariff on Canadian autos, sliding the Canadian dollar and forcing Ottawa to prepare a Tuesday response. The common thread is a liquidity-driven bid for gold and short-dollar positions colliding with fresh tariff and sanctions risk, leaving oil and energy-importing Asian FX as the most sensitive cross-asset vectors.
