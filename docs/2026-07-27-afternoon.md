> [!abstract] Overview
> Singapore’s central bank delivered a surprise back-to-back tightening, lifting the Singapore dollar across the board as it flagged durable inflation risks from the Middle East oil shock and the AI boom. The move overshadowed the sudden resignation of Bank Indonesia Governor Perry Warjiyo, which rattled the rupiah and revived long-standing concerns about policy independence under President Prabowo’s administration. Chinese chip champion CXMT soared 466% in its Shanghai debut, underscoring a retail frenzy for domestic semiconductor stories even as industrial profit growth slipped to its weakest pace this year and Shein slumped to a loss ahead of its Hong Kong IPO. The intraday policy activism in Asia, together with a larger risk-on mood following the US‑Iran pause, kept global equities and bonds supported ahead of back-to-back central bank decisions this week.

## 🔬 Narrative Watch

### 📌 Singapore tightens monetary policy for second straight meeting, strengthening the SGD

**Fact**
The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) said it would increase the rate of appreciation of the local dollar’s trade-weighted value, tightening monetary policy for the second time in three months ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/southeast-asia/article/3361951/singapore-tightens-monetary-policy-second-time-3-months)). The move comes as oil prices remain elevated owing to tensions in the Middle East, sparked by the US-Israel attacks on Iran that began on February 28, and continue to cast a shadow ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/southeast-asia/article/3361951/singapore-tightens-monetary-policy-second-time-3-months)). Singapore’s central bank tightened monetary policy for a second straight meeting as the Middle East conflict and the boom in artificial intelligence are expected to spur inflationary pressures for longer than expected ([BBG](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/singapore-tightens-monetary-policy-further-on-price-pressures)). The MAS said the economy would stay firm for the rest of 2026 as the AI boom cushions oil shocks and the new US tariff ([CNA](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMirwFBVV95cUxOTXBISDNUc1luX1RNcGV5WWE5VFF3aGxSc19FdU9GNGE5V1FmaGVoTG51Wm5OaG1reWl4SEZqNTlUOVlQV0oxeDVqYVpjMl9wNXc5bmhYVTdyQ3NZVmdvd3FVQ1NfYTl1ZWxEZ29iOGc1bkhLQXhGVXhlZmQ5bC1CV0lSZUNaaHd5RkJqTmt2RndBaWZmV1I1MlRORHZjVHFrNG14SlZ6YTYxSGRTcUJz)).

> [!info] [AI Reasoning]
> * **Narrative change**: Acceleration — the second straight tightening with explicit oil/AI inflation drivers transforms the MAS stance from one-off to a determined hiking cycle.
> * **Transmission / Market Read**: The policy channel and capital-flow channel support a stronger SGD; favors **long SGD** against regional peers, particularly **short IDR** and **short MYR** on policy divergence.
> * **Watchpoint / Confidence**: Next MAS statement in October; any further oil spike or core CPI surprise would validate persistence. Confidence: High thanks to official statement and multiple cross-source confirmations.

### 📌 Bank Indonesia Governor Warjiyo unexpectedly resigns, rattling the rupiah

**Fact**
Bank Indonesia Governor Perry Warjiyo suddenly resigned for unspecified “personal reasons,” unnerving investors who had viewed the central banker as a stabilizing force in an economy whipsawed by the policies of President Prabowo Subianto ([BBG 1](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/bank-indonesia-chief-warjiyo-resigns-early-deputy-to-take-over)). Destry Damayanti, announced as acting chief of Bank Indonesia Monday, is a trained economist whose monetary policy experience may comfort investors rattled by the sudden resignation ([BBG 2](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/indonesia-s-acting-central-bank-chief-damayanti-seen-as-reassuring-for-investors)). Warjiyo’s departure is expected to heighten uncertainty and volatility across the nation’s financial markets, with analysts citing concerns over policy direction and central bank independence ([BBG 3](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-27/warjiyo-s-departure-raises-scrutiny-on-bi-policy-analysts-say)). Warjiyo had been under pressure over the weakening rupiah ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/f9abab6b-964c-4dae-ba41-4c20fa71c2e0)).

> [!info] [AI Reasoning]
> * **Narrative change**: New — the resignation of a credible governor raises political risk around BI’s autonomy, a fresh shock for EM investors already navigating volatile oil and trade policy.
> * **Transmission / Market Read**: The risk-premium and capital-flow channels tilt toward **short IDR**; any further deprecation could trigger ad‑hoc intervention or an emergency rate hike.
> * **Watchpoint / Confidence**: Watch Damayanti’s first policy statement and any explicit pledge to maintain independence; a formal parliamentary confirmation of a permanent governor would be the main invalidation trigger. Confidence: Medium (high cross‑source agreement but the ultimate policy path remains uncertain).

### 📌 China’s CXMT soars 466% on debut, becoming mainland’s most valuable listed company

**Fact**
ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT), China’s leading maker of dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) chips, saw its shares close up 466 percent on their Shanghai trading debut, giving the company a market capitalisation of 3.28 trillion yuan (US$484.6 billion) and making it mainland China’s most valuable onshore listing ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3361926/chinas-cxmt-shares-rise-472-star-market-debut-valuing-dram-maker-us489-billion)). The semiconductor champion’s Monday debut saw unprecedented trading volume, with turnover exceeding 140 billion yuan as CXMT became the first mainland-listed stock to cross the 100 billion yuan single-day threshold ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3361926/chinas-cxmt-shares-rise-472-star-market-debut-valuing-dram-maker-us489-billion)). The flotation marked the mainland’s biggest IPO since 2010 ([FT](https://www.ft.com/content/8e82e939-908b-42bf-a314-0bb02a3f1b07)).

> [!info] [AI Reasoning]
> * **Narrative change**: Confirmation — the blockbuster debut reinforces the onshore rotation into semis and domestic tech champions, deepening the equity‑market split between AI‑driven sectors and the rest of the economy.
> * **Transmission / Market Read**: The liquidity and risk‑appetite channel supports **China onshore equities** in the near term, but the extreme concentration risk may amplify a correction if the AI narrative falters; limited direct FX transmission.
> * **Watchpoint / Confidence**: The first post‑IPO lock‑up expiry and any official cooling measures from the CSRC will test the retail bid. Confidence: High (the price action is directly observable and widely reported).
