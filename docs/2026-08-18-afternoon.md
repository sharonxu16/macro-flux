> [!abstract] Overview
> Oil’s post-ceasefire squeeze hardened into a broader supply and rates shock on Tuesday, with Brent topping `$91` and Iran signaling the Strait of Hormuz would stay shut until Washington met interim-deal conditions, while China’s state shippers repositioned tankers outside the Gulf. Long-end yields led the repricing, as the US 30-year approached `5.32%` and German and Japanese benchmarks hit multi-decade or multiyear peaks. The shock now extends from commodities into sovereign curves, with Washington’s decision to cut joint South Korea drills and an open Kim channel injecting a fresh Asian security risk premium just as China’s onshore yields slide to a 13-month low and Beijing trims its Treasury stockpile. The dollar is torn between fading Fed hike bets and war-driven demand, while Asian allies face deteriorating terms on both energy imports and security assurance. The common thread is an accelerating supply-side inflation shock that feeds directly into term premia and forces a repricing of every energy-importing and security-dependent economy.
