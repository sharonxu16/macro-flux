> [!abstract] Overview
> Overnight macro was dominated by a much stronger-than-expected US August payrolls print of `162,000` jobs against a `53,000` consensus, pushing short-end Treasury yields higher and lifting market pricing for a September Federal Reserve hike. President Donald Trump immediately counterpunched, threatening to halt trade with countries running US deficits unless the Fed cuts rates, while Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack said it is time to act on inflation. The Iran war kept the energy risk premium elevated, with US diesel at a record high, hedge funds the most bullish on Brent since May, the EU joining sanctions, and South Korea weighing a military role to reopen Hormuz. China rare earth firms halted some US shipments, reviving the minerals front just as US and Chinese military commanders resumed direct talks. The connecting thread is a collision between labor-driven rate repricing and war-driven energy inflation, keeping upward pressure on US yields and downside pressure on Asian FX.
