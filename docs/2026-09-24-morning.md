> [!abstract] Overview
> A violent Treasury selloff drove the 10-year yield through 5.1% and the dollar to an eight-week high after hot US activity and inflation data forced markets to price an October Fed hike. Against that tightening impulse, Washington and Beijing extended the Busan trade truce to Jan. 10, removing a near-term tariff cliff as Xi Jinping began his state visit. But the Middle East supply picture deteriorated again, with a fatal attack on a cargo ship in Hormuz and a defiant Iranian president lifting oil and keeping Asia import inflation risks alive. US equities and long-duration assets fell, while Japan and Asia FX face pressure from both US nominal tightening and energy risk. The connective thread: US nominal tightening is the dominant force, with the China trade truce a partial offset and oil supply risk an inflation wildcard.
