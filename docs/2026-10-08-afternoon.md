> [!abstract] Overview
> Middle East escalation led Asian risk assets lower in the afternoon as Brent crude broke above `$103` and the Houthis claimed a ballistic missile attack on Riyadh airport, reviving the energy-risk premium just as the dollar held near an 18-month high. European sovereign stress deepened further, with bank shares sliding and French government credit trading behind a large share of France’s corporate debt, while traders positioned for continued euro weakness. China-EU trade talks opened in Beijing with both sides signalling little room for compromise, reinforcing soft-growth and tariff exposure for European autos and Asian supply chains. Against that, AI demand stayed strong with TSMC’s third-quarter revenue at a record and Samsung guiding to its first 100 trillion won operating profit, but broad Asian equities fell, reportedly led by a `2.6%` drop in the KOSPI on oil-driven inflation fears. The connecting thread remains a tightening dollar-liquidity and energy-shock squeeze that punishes energy importers, European credit, and EM carry currencies.
