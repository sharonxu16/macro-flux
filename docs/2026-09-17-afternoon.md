> [!abstract] Overview
> Markets reversed part of the overnight hawkish shock as the dollar eased from its peak, Treasury yields pulled back and US equity futures climbed, with gold rebounding more than 2% and oil down 3% to a one-week low on easing Middle East supply fears. The Fed's first hike since 2023 is now being framed less by Trump confrontation and more by market trust in Chair Warsh's inflation fight, while the Bank of Japan faces a higher bar to support the yen into Friday's decision. China's US Treasury holdings fell to the lowest since 2008 and its youth unemployment rate rose to 18.9%, reinforcing structural stress just before Xi's Washington summit. Hong Kong unveiled its first five-year plan to deepen offshore yuan and capital market links. The cross-market thread is a hawkish dollar bloc against a China that is de-dollarising and weakening, with oil and European gas still adding inflation tail risk.
