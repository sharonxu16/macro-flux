> [!abstract] Overview
> Federal Reserve tightening expectations remain the dominant macro driver: officials are described as poised to raise rates despite the tech-led sell-off, the dollar is on track for its longest monthly winning run since 2024, and Canada’s Big Six banks now all price a December hike. Oil adds a second pressure point after Yemen’s Houthi rebels struck a key Saudi refinery, Middle East energy infrastructure attacks intensified, and at least two workers were killed in Kuwait, putting crude on course for its first weekly gain this month. Europe adds policy uncertainty as Spain’s Pablo Hernández de Cos wins leaders’ nod to succeed Christine Lagarde at the ECB, while the October accounts show officials saw worsening inflation and the need for another hike unless the outlook improved significantly, and the euro is set for its biggest drop against the yuan in two decades. China’s Ministry of Commerce pushed back against US Section 301 tariffs as Washington’s new levies on 14 partners force trading partners toward deals, but markets continue to treat the latest round as less dire. The cross-market thread is a hawkish DM central bank plus geopolitical supply risk that supports the dollar and commodities while keeping Asian FX on the defensive.
