> [!abstract] Overview
> China's October activity data surprised to the downside across the board, with investment unexpectedly worsening while consumption and industrial output undershot estimates, signaling that stimulus has yet to feed through. The disappointment drove iron ore futures to a four-year low and copper to a four-month low, and the PBOC reinforced the cautious tone by draining medium-term funds for the first time in five months to push back on easing bets. Japan compounded the regional growth concern as third-quarter GDP contracted unexpectedly for the first time in a year, squeezed by inflation and expired energy subsidies, even as BOJ hawk Takata called for a swift move to a good rate level. Geopolitics remained front and center: Beijing confirmed Thursday's Xi-Trump-Putin trilateral in Shenzhen, US allies signed an accord to keep China out of AI supply chains, and the US deployed a third aircraft carrier to the Middle East amid fresh Hormuz tanker incidents. The cross-market thread is a strong dollar plus a weak China-centered Asian growth pulse pressuring industrial commodities and Asian assets, with summit and Gulf risk premium layered on top.
