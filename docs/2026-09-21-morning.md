> [!abstract] Overview
> A fresh attack on Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline has shut the key Hormuz bypass and forced Aramco to halt October crude deliveries to some European refiners, adding a physical supply shock to oil already above `US$100/bbl` and prompting France to call a G7 meeting on releasing reserves. Friday’s Fed hike backdrop kept the `10-year Treasury` near `5%`, with the `2-year yield` hitting a new multiyear high as Fed speakers warned inflation has broadened beyond energy. US-China summit preparations accelerated: Washington signed new Russia sanctions legislation with sweeping tariff powers days before Xi’s visit, while US and Chinese officials opened talks on AI, trade, critical minerals and possible LNG tariff relief. The BOJ raised rates to the highest since 1995 yet the yen still sank toward `160` as traders doubted the pace of tightening, with thin Japanese holiday liquidity and intervention warnings adding a sharp FX tail. North Korea fired two missiles within three hours over US drill tensions, and cross-strait signals were mixed with Taiwan sending a rare minister to mainland APEC while Washington weighed delaying arms sales. The weekend’s common thread is supply-shock inflation pressure colliding with a make-or-break US-China summit and two newly hawkish central banks.

### 📌 Saudi East-West pipeline attack deepens oil supply shock

**Fact**
The shutdown of Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline removed a key escape route from the disruption in the Strait of Hormuz, deepening an energy shock that has already pushed crude oil above `US$100` a barrel ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/week-asia/economics/article/3368062/saudi-oil-pipeline-closure-puts-asias-economic-resilience-test)). Analysts said the loss of that bypass would hit Asian refiners first before feeding through to inflation, currencies and economic growth across the region ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/week-asia/economics/article/3368062/saudi-oil-pipeline-closure-puts-asias-economic-resilience-test)). Aramco has halted October crude deliveries to some European refiners after the pipeline attack, Bloomberg reports ([Reuters](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMixgFBVV95cUxPLXZOci1kaFhqMWtjVXZMN1BDS0Q0LUF0UjNEVUlIMHlJR2x0bkNvZlhnX3FSQXg1T0lyRlVHSDlIV09xOFBOdUFSWU4yMmpMOUE5aXpJYjdJaGNZVTdwVmdxWlZuNmd1OU5hUXh5clNDbHh0dFNTQVJRaUdJdGNfdk4ySE1NVXZtODQ1UENyczdUMGpTTzNBaG82bjBvSm94WGxLZFNIOXFBQy1xb0Zqb0d6cVRISG52UkNSOXc0aGJROUgzLUE)). Oil steadied as traders tracked supply risks in the Middle East, and intensified diplomatic efforts to end the US-Iran war ([BBG](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-20/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-sept-21)). Qatar Energy Minister Saad al-Kaabi said “to call the strait obsolete is ‘wrong’” ([BBG](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/videos/2026-09-20/hormuz-cannot-be-obsolete-qatar-energy-minister-says-video)).
