> [!abstract] Overview
> A violent sovereign bond selloff pushed the US 30-year yield to a new 19-year high and dragged semiconductor and tech shares lower from the US through Europe and Asia. The Hormuz shutdown hardened as Iran said the strait will remain closed until Washington meets interim deal conditions, while President Trump said no talks are scheduled, keeping crude bid for a fourth day. Korea was the sharpest regional stress point, with the KOSPI triggering a circuit breaker after dropping more than 6% intraday as Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi began a Seoul visit against a backdrop of reduced US-Korea drills. China is moving in the opposite direction, with long-end yields falling and the curve flattening aggressively versus a global repricing that has lifted borrowing costs across major economies. The common thread is an energy-driven inflation and sovereign debt shock tightening global financial conditions while forcing Asia to price both US alliance risk and domestic easing.
