> [!abstract] Overview
> The Federal Reserve’s decision to hold rates steady alongside the most dramatic hawkish dissent in a decade has pushed the 30-year Treasury yield through `5.2%` for the first time since 2007, leaving markets to price a dangerous combination of policy inertia and an intensifying Iran-driven oil shock. Crude surged nearly `8%` after the US launched fresh strikes inside Iran and a drone hit a gas tanker at an Egyptian Mediterranean port, crushing the fragile diplomatic track that had briefly buoyed risk appetite. The Kospi’s continued slide and South Korea’s emergency move to restrict leveraged ETF access underscore a deepening tech rout that is now forcing a government-level response. The macro thread connecting these moves is a singular repricing of the central bank put: a divided Fed facing a supply‑side oil spike is fueling the worst bear steepening in a generation, tightening financial conditions just as Asia’s most leveraged equity trades unravel.
